2. Where are you posting?

The primary keyword is what the SEO score is measured against - whether it appears before the "see more" fold, how often it repeats, and whether the alt text carries it. Leave it blank and those checks are skipped rather than guessed.

3. Have a model draft them optional

The scoring is always on and needs no key - it grades whatever is in the caption box, whether a model wrote it or you did. What a key buys you is the first draft. Leave this off and you get editable scaffolds instead: the right structure with the keyword already placed, for you to fill in. A scaffold you complete beats a caption a model invented.

    This report is not saved. It lives in this tab's memory only. Reloading or closing destroys it. Copy what you need or use Export Markdown first.

    What is measured and what is judged. Sharpness (variance of the Laplacian), exposure and highlight clipping, contrast, colourfulness, horizon tilt, background clutter, subject placement and near-duplicate distance are measured from your pixels - real numbers, shown next to every verdict so you can disagree with the weighting rather than a black box. The 1-10 scores and the winner are judged: they apply documented thresholds and per-platform weights to those numbers. That is a defensible heuristic, not ground truth, and it says nothing about what a photo is of. Only the optional caption writer understands subject matter.

    What the caption score is. Every check is a rule applied to your text: does the keyword appear before the fold, how many hashtags, how long is the hook, is there an ask, is that a cliché. It runs locally on whatever is in the box, so it grades your own writing exactly as it grades a model's. What it is not is a prediction. It has no access to what is trending, no view counts, no idea how your audience behaves - a page with no live data that claimed otherwise would just be inventing numbers. A high score means the caption obeys the rules that usually help; it does not promise reach.

    Data policy. With captions off, nothing leaves your browser - there is no server behind this page. With captions on, the chosen photo and its measurements are sent to whichever provider you picked (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic or NVIDIA); that is a real hop, and "we don't store it" is a claim about this page, not about every provider in the chain. Their retention and training terms are theirs, not mine to promise - read them if the photos are sensitive. Nothing here writes to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB or cookies, ever.
